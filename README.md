# Uncovering-Manipulated-Files-Using-Mathematical-Natural-Laws
This work proposes an innovative methodology for the detection of manipulated PDF files, based on Benford’s Law and the statistical analysis of numerical patterns. The model is grounded on the premise that benign files tend to conform to Benford’s Law, whereas tampered or malicious files exhibit statistically significant deviations from the expected empirical frequency distribution.

The proposed approach was validated using the PDFMALWARE dataset, demonstrating that the application of natural mathematical laws can be effective in identifying inconsistencies and anomalies that often evade conventional detection techniques. The study stands out not only for its unconventional perspective, but also for its innovative application of a set of dissimilarity measures, namely the Pearson and Spearman correlation coefficients, as well as the Crámer–von Mises test, in an initial analysis phase.

In order to improve the robustness of the p-values obtained from the Pearson and Spearman correlation coefficients, the Mean Absolute Deviation (MAD) was employed as a normalization mechanism, reducing sensitivity to extreme variations and enhancing the reliability of the results.

Beyond providing a robust solution that is independent of machine learning methods, particularly suitable for scenarios involving imbalanced or limited data, this study opens new avenues for the application of mathematical statistics, combined with dissimilarity functions, in the fields of cybersecurity and digital forensics, with direct implications for fraud and malware detection in digital documents.
